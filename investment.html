03 / THE BUSINESS MODEL

Test the value before
setting the price.

Recurring access and usage-based stages are business model hypotheses. Customer evidence should determine the right mix.

SUBSCRIPTION HYPOTHESIS

A recurring workspace.

A monthly or annual SaaS plan for teams evaluating ideas regularly, with project history and collaboration. Test renewal intent and repeated use.

USAGE HYPOTHESIS

Pay for deeper validation.

Stage credits or a phase fee could align the bill with research and agent costs. Test whether buyers prefer predictable allowances or flexible usage.

ECONOMIC VALIDATION

Measure the cost to serve.

Track model and data costs per completed stage, support time, paid retention, and gross margin in actual pilots before scaling acquisition.

No pricing, contracted revenue, commercial traction, or unit economics are claimed in this brief.

05 / MILESTONES & VALIDATION

Earn the next step.

Proposed gates make the progress reviewable. Targets below are pilot design goals, not results already achieved.

MONTHS 1–3

Confirm the buyer.

Interview at least 15 prospective buyers and run five supervised prototype sessions. Identify one repeated use case and its current alternative.

Gate: A defined buyer, a measurable decision problem, and three willing design partners. Rework the segment if the need is weak.

MONTHS 4–6

Prove the workflow.

Build the first real orchestration path and run five end-to-end pilot projects. Compare the outputs with source evidence and human assessment.

Gate: At least four pilot projects complete with acceptable review quality; job reliability and cost per stage are documented.

MONTHS 7–9

Test payment.

Offer a bounded paid pilot to ten qualified prospects. Compare subscription and stage-based proposals, and record purchase objections.

Gate: At least three paid pilots and evidence of repeat use. Revisit the proposition if buyers will use it but will not pay.

MONTHS 10–12

Decide whether to scale.

Review paid cohort usage, retention intent, support demand, and cost to serve. Choose a focused launch segment or another validation cycle.

Gate: A documented go / no-go decision based on pilot economics, output quality, and customer value.

06 / RISKS TO RESOLVE

The questions that
matter before scale.

Reliable judgment

Several agents can share the same bias or produce convincing but unsupported conclusions.

Response: Attributable sources, disagreement review, evaluation benchmarks, and human approval at consequential decisions.

Usage economics

Multi-agent research can cost more than a customer is willing to pay.

Response: Measure each stage, set budgets and retries, and test packaging before expanding paid acquisition.

Willingness to pay

A useful one-off exercise may not create a recurring buying habit.

Response: Start with repeat evaluators, observe real workflows, and make paid pilots a validation gate.

Trust & sensitive data

Commercial briefs can include information that users do not want shared or retained.

Response: Design access controls, retention choices, and provider policies before handling production customer data.
